A tropical fishing holiday is all about long days on the water under a strong sun, so what you pack matters. Forget the sunglasses or the right leader and you'll feel it on day one. This packing list covers everything to take on a tropical fishing trip: tackle, clothes, sun protection, health and travel essentials, with tips specific to a fishing holiday in the Maldives.

Fishing tackle: what to bring and what's provided

Start by checking what the boat provides. On our private charter boat in the Maldives, Emperor, basic fishing gear is included on every trip, and you can add a professional popping or jigging rod and reel setup for USD 80 per setup. Trolling tackle for tuna and wahoo is supplied by the boat. Many keen anglers still bring their own popping and jigging outfits because they know and trust them.

If you're bringing your own tackle

  • Popping outfit: a heavy GT popping rod and a large saltwater spinning reel loaded with heavy braid.
  • Jigging outfit: a jigging rod and reel matched to speed or slow-pitch jigging.
  • Lures: poppers and stickbaits in a few sizes, and metal jigs in several weights.
  • Terminal tackle: spare leader material, strong split rings, solid rings, single hooks and assist hooks.
  • Tools: pliers, split-ring pliers, line cutters and a small repair kit.
  • Fighting gear: a gimbal or fighting belt and a pair of fishing gloves.
  • Rod tube: a hard travel tube for rods; multi-piece travel rods are easier on flights.

Clothing for a fishing trip in the tropics

  • Long-sleeved fishing shirts with UV protection: lighter and cooler than you think, and they save your arms from burning.
  • Quick-dry shorts or light trousers.
  • A buff or neck gaiter to cover your neck and face.
  • A wide-brimmed hat or cap, ideally with a neck flap or a clip so it doesn't blow away.
  • Non-marking deck shoes or sandals with grip for a wet deck.
  • A light rain jacket or windbreaker for squalls, early starts and boat rides.
  • Swimwear for the beach and the boat.
  • Modest clothes for local islands (see Maldives tips below).

Sun protection

The tropical sun reflects off the water, so you burn faster on a boat than on land. Pack:

  • Polarised sunglasses, the most important item after your tackle: they cut glare and help you see fish following a lure. Bring a spare pair and a retainer strap.
  • High-factor, reef-safe sunscreen, and reapply it through the day.
  • Lip balm with SPF.
  • Sun gloves to protect the backs of your hands.
  • After-sun lotion for the evening.

Health and comfort

  • Seasickness tablets or bands, even if you don't usually get seasick. Offshore water can be lumpy.
  • A reusable water bottle. Drink plenty on the boat.
  • A small first-aid kit: plasters, antiseptic, painkillers, and something for hook cuts and line burns.
  • Insect repellent for evenings on the island.
  • Any prescription medicines, in their original packaging.

Travel and electronics

  • Passport, travel insurance that covers fishing and boat trips, and booking confirmations.
  • A waterproof phone case or dry bag for the boat.
  • A camera or action camera with a float strap.
  • A power bank and charging cables.
  • Some cash in US dollars for small purchases and tips.

Maldives packing tips

  • Dress modestly on local islands. The Maldives is a Muslim country. On local islands, cover shoulders and knees away from the beach, and wear swimwear only on designated tourist beaches and on the boat.
  • Don't pack alcohol. Bringing alcohol into the Maldives is not allowed, and it is confiscated at the airport. Pork products are also restricted.
  • Check domestic flight baggage limits. Getting to the southern atolls means a domestic flight, which may have a lower baggage allowance than your international flight. Ask about rod tubes and extra weight when you book.
  • Bring US dollars. They are widely accepted alongside the local rufiyaa.
  • Pack light for a lodge stay. At the Maldives Fishing and Holidays Lodge on Maamendhoo, life is simple: fishing clothes, island clothes and your tackle are all you need.

What not to pack

  • Heavy cotton clothes. They stay wet and hot; quick-dry fabrics are better on a boat.
  • Black-soled shoes. They mark the deck; choose non-marking soles.
  • Too much tackle. Check what the boat supplies first, and bring the outfits and lures you'll really use.
  • Glass bottles and single-use plastics. The islands have limited waste disposal; a refillable bottle is kinder to the reef.

Fishing holiday packing: FAQs

Do I need to bring my own fishing gear to the Maldives?

Not on our charters: basic gear is included, trolling tackle is supplied, and a professional popping or jigging setup is USD 80 per setup. Bring your own if you prefer your own outfit.

What should I wear on a fishing boat?

A long-sleeved UV fishing shirt, quick-dry shorts, a hat, a buff, polarised sunglasses and non-marking shoes with grip.

Can I bring fishing rods on a domestic flight in the Maldives?

Usually, yes, but domestic flights can have lower baggage limits. Pack rods in a hard tube and check the allowance for long items when you book.

What is the most important thing to pack for a fishing trip?

After your tackle, polarised sunglasses and serious sun protection. The sun on the water is strong, and sunglasses help you spot fish.
