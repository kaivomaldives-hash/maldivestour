The Maldives has hundreds of boats offering fishing trips, from resort speedboats to local dhonis and dedicated sport fishing boats. The operator you choose decides whether you spend the day on the right reef at the right tide, or trolling aimlessly near the resort. This checklist explains how to choose a fishing charter in the Maldives: what to look for in the boat, the crew and the safety equipment, how to compare prices, and the questions to ask before you book.

1. Decide what kind of trip you want

Start with your goal, because it narrows the choice:

  • A relaxed evening and a few reef fish: a shared sunset handline trip from a local island is enough.
  • A specific species, such as GT, tuna or dogtooth tuna: you need a private charter with a boat and crew set up for popping, jigging or trolling.
  • Several days of serious fishing: look for an operator that offers fishing packages with accommodation on or near the fishing grounds.

See private vs shared fishing charters for the differences.

2. Check the operator is registered

A proper operator is a registered company with a name, a registration number and contact details it is happy to share. Ask who operates the boat, whether they are registered in the Maldives, and who you are paying. Be wary of anyone who can't tell you.

For example, our charters are run by Maldives Fishing and Holiday Pvt Ltd, a registered Maldivian company on Maamendhoo, Gaafu Alifu Atoll, which runs both the boat and the Maldives Fishing and Holidays Lodge.

3. Look at the boat

  • Size and capacity. How long is the boat and how many anglers does it take? For popping, everyone needs room to cast; four or five anglers is a comfortable maximum on a mid-sized sport fishing boat.
  • Engines. Twin engines give more range and a backup if one fails, which matters when you're far offshore.
  • Layout. A centre-console boat with an open deck is ideal for popping and jigging; a boat with rod holders and outriggers suits trolling.
  • Shade and comfort. A T-top or canopy, seating and somewhere to store bags make a long day easier.
  • Real photos. Ask for photos of the actual boat you'll be on, not stock images.

Emperor, our boat, is a 32-foot centre-console fishing boat with twin 200 HP engines and room for up to 5 anglers.

4. Ask about safety equipment

Before you book, ask the operator what safety equipment is on board. A well-run charter boat should be able to tell you about:

  • life jackets for everyone on board, including children's sizes if needed
  • a radio and a way to call for help offshore
  • a first-aid kit
  • navigation equipment
  • how they decide whether it's safe to go out in bad weather

A good operator answers these questions happily. If they don't, choose another.

5. Judge the crew

  • Local knowledge. The best captains know which reefs and channels fish on which tide. Ask where they fish and how they plan the day.
  • Technique. If you want to pop or jig, check the crew does it regularly, not just trolling.
  • Communication. Can they explain what they're doing and help beginners?
  • Catch and release. Ask how they handle and release fish such as GT and billfish.

6. Compare prices properly

Fishing prices in the Maldives are quoted in different ways, so compare like with like:

  • Per boat or per person? Private charters are usually per boat; shared trips per person.
  • How long? Check the hours on the water, not just "half day" or "full day".
  • What's included? Captain, crew, fuel, drinks, snacks and tackle can all be extras.
  • Transfers? Are you picked up from your island or resort, or do you need to get to the boat?
  • Taxes and service charges? Ask whether the price is final.

7. Check the tackle

Ask what tackle is supplied and whether it's suitable for the fishing you want. Basic gear is fine for reef fishing and trolling, but GT popping and jigging need heavy, specialised rods, reels, line and lures. Find out whether you can rent a professional setup or should bring your own.

8. Ask about weather and cancellations

Weather can stop a trip, especially in the southwest monsoon. Ask what happens if the captain decides it's unsafe to go out: will the trip be moved to another day, refunded, or shortened? If you're booking several days, ask how they handle a lost day.

9. Read reviews and look at real catches

Look for recent reviews and photos of real catches from the operator's own boat. Be cautious about photos that appear on many different websites, or catch claims that sound too good to be true. A good operator will tell you honestly what is likely at the time of year you're travelling.

Questions to ask before you book a fishing charter

  1. Who operates the boat, and is the company registered?
  2. How big is the boat, how many engines does it have, and how many anglers does it take?
  3. What safety equipment is on board?
  4. Is the price per boat or per person, and what exactly is included?
  5. How many hours will we be on the water?
  6. What techniques do you fish, and what can we realistically catch this month?
  7. What tackle do you supply, and can I rent a professional setup?
  8. Where does the trip start, and are transfers included?
  9. What happens if the weather is bad?
  10. How do you handle catch and release?

Warning signs

  • No company name or registration, or payment to a personal account with no paperwork.
  • Prices that are far below everyone else's, with no clear list of what's included.
  • Only stock photos, or the same catch photos used by other companies.
  • Promises of guaranteed catches.
  • Vague answers about safety equipment or bad-weather policy.

Choosing a fishing charter: FAQs

How do I find a good fishing charter in the Maldives?

Choose a registered operator with a suitable boat and an experienced local crew, ask about safety equipment, compare prices on the same basis (per boat, hours, inclusions), and look for recent reviews and real catch photos.

Should I book a fishing trip through my resort?

It's convenient, but resort trips are often more expensive and limited to nearby waters. For a serious fishing day, a dedicated sport fishing charter usually offers more.

How much should a private fishing charter cost in the Maldives?

It varies by boat, length and inclusions. Emperor costs USD 980 per boat for a half day or USD 1,380 for a full day, for up to 5 anglers, including the crew, fuel, drinks, snacks and basic gear.

Is it safe to go fishing in the Maldives?

With a registered operator, a well-equipped boat and an experienced captain who decides when the weather is suitable, fishing trips in the Maldives are a normal part of holidays for many visitors. Always ask about safety equipment before you book.
